Q4 readiness optimizations

  1. Pre-peak account review. Cost per click rose to $20.90 in October. In early November we review search terms, the 100% target ROAS setting and assets so the December budget goes to the strongest queries.
  2. Brand and general search separation. Protect searches for the Lustgarten name at low cost, and budget general cancer research giving searches on their own results.
  3. Seasonal messaging. World Pancreatic Cancer Day (November 19), Giving Tuesday (December 1) and year-end assets ready ahead of each date.
  4. Mobile donation experience. Mobile converts at 12.9% vs 22.5% on desktop. A page speed and form length review could lift results from the larger mobile audience.
  5. Weekly pacing. Budget moved between campaigns based on cost per donation through December.

The pilot tests a different donor segment, such as adult children and family members of people affected by pancreatic cancer, rather than duplicating Google Ads. Results are judged on new donor share as well as cost per donation.

Why it fits

  • Ads match conversational intent: people comparing organizations or deciding where to give.
  • Context hints guide placement. They are not exact-match keywords.
  • A clear research message ($333 million invested in research) stands out when donors compare charities.
  • Ads are labeled as sponsored and do not influence ChatGPT's answers.

Guardrails

  • No ads in sensitive contexts, including personal health conversations. Ads are not shown to someone describing a diagnosis.
  • Health advertising is reviewed case by case in the US, and nonprofit fundraising is not specifically addressed, so eligibility is confirmed with OpenAI first.
  • Copy stays factual, with no medical claims.

Policy first: health-related rules on Meta

Policy areaWhat it meansHow we plan for it
Data classificationMeta may classify the site as health-related, which can limit website audiences, block donation optimization and remove URL parameters.Check Events Manager in Phase 0. Prepare a full and a restricted setup.
Personal attributesAds cannot imply the viewer has a condition. "Diagnosed with pancreatic cancer?" is not allowed.Talk about the cause, the research and other people's stories, never the viewer as a patient.
NamingAudiences or conversions named after health traits can be disabled.Neutral names. Donor lists only with permissions.
Health claimsNo unsupported medical claims or promised outcomes.Focus on hope, motivation and research funding.
TargetingNo detailed targeting based on health topics.Broad or Advantage+ audiences with location and age settings.
Social issue adsMeta may request authorization and a "Paid for by" disclaimer.Fundraising focus. Authorization completed if requested.

Phase 1 audiences

  • Website visitors, last 30 to 60 days, excluding donors. May be limited by the classification.
  • Facebook page and Instagram engagers. Available in every scenario.
  • Video viewers. Available in every scenario, and the bridge to Phase 2.
  • Existing donor list, as an exclusion or for year-end renewal messages, with permissions.

Phase 2 video concepts

  • A family member on why they give in honor or memory of a loved one.
  • A Lustgarten-funded researcher on what their work aims to achieve.
  • A Walk for Research participant or long-time donor on why they support the cause.
  • A short explainer on how donations fund research.

15 to 30 seconds, vertical and square, captioned. Signed releases from everyone featured.

Do

  • Stories told by the speaker, or in the third person
  • Hope, motivation and research funding
  • A strong first three seconds and a clear Donate call to action

Avoid

  • Speaking to the viewer as if they or family have cancer
  • Claims about cures, survival odds or treatment results
  • Graphic medical imagery or hospital scenes
